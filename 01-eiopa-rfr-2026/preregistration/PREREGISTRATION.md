## 1. The question
From the published documentation alone, can two blind builders of different model families reproduce the text's own worked examples — and where they cannot, which passages caused it, on what grounds?

This is a replicability test: two independent implementations of a public text, each set against the publisher's own worked examples. It makes no regulatory-accountability claim of any kind, and it attributes no error to the text's owner (§11).

## 5. Pass or fail — per declared output
- Every declared output cell is compared with its official value; a case passes for a builder when every one of its declared outputs passes.
- **The pass bands**, set before any build from the oracle's own precision:
  - the curves the workbook rounds to 5 decimals — A1 L25:L174, A2 L24:L173, A3 L23:L172: |builder − official| ≤ 1.0 × 10⁻⁵, one unit in the fifth decimal. It absorbs the workbook's rounding, which the text does not state for the curves, and the start of A2's and A3's curves from A1's rounded curve, which the text states neither way (§6);
  - the VA values the workbook rounds to 4 decimals — A2 H10, A3 L13; the text states the VA in integer basis points: |builder − official| < 0.5 × 10⁻⁴, half a basis point;
  - every unrounded output of A1–A3 — A1 K25:K74 and L22; A2 L16 and K20; A3 L10, L11 and K20: |builder − official| ≤ 1.0 × 10⁻⁹, far below any rounding the text states; the workbook solves its bootstrap to |g| < 10⁻¹⁵;
  - B1, B2 and C1 rest on internal effective rates the workbooks set with Excel Solver, whose own residuals — squared value gaps up to 2.8 × 10⁻⁸ in E2 and 9.05 × 10⁻⁸ in E3 — leave the oracle short of the exact root. Each of their outputs gets the largest change those residuals allow in it — each rate's change the larger of its residual's reach through the rate's sensitivity and its exact root's departure, summed through the output's formula with absolute coefficients — computed from the workbooks by a published tool (`preregistration/tools/bands.py`) and rounded up to two significant digits: B1 (EUR) — the corporate portfolio's S 2.8 × 10⁻⁵ and RC 4.2 × 10⁻⁵, S 1.7 × 10⁻⁵, RC 2.6 × 10⁻⁵, RCS 8.6 × 10⁻⁶; B2 (IT) — 3.7 × 10⁻⁵, 5.5 × 10⁻⁵, 1.5 × 10⁻⁵, 2.2 × 10⁻⁵, 7.4 × 10⁻⁶; C1 (GBP) — 8.0 × 10⁻⁵, 1.1 × 10⁻⁴, 6.2 × 10⁻⁵, 7.9 × 10⁻⁵, 1.8 × 10⁻⁵; the normalised weights and the government portfolio's S and RC, which no residual reaches beyond 10⁻¹¹ — 1.0 × 10⁻⁹. The exact root of each workbook equation lies inside these bands for all 27 outputs.
- An output missing, non-numeric or given for another cell fails.
- The pass-or-fail table is produced by the comparison tool, whose code carries a second party's check before its result is relied on; the table records the tool's sha256 and the command that produced it.

## 6. Divergences and their attribution
- **A divergence** is a declared output outside its band. The divergence log is generated from the table — every failing output enters; none is chosen.
- **Each divergence is attributed on stated grounds, or left unresolved:**
  - **T — a passage of the text:** silent, ambiguous or self-inconsistent at the point where the builder's code departs; grounds — the passage by section and paragraph, the readings it allows, and the builder's reading as its code shows it;
  - **O — the oracle departs from the text:** the text, read in full, states a rule at the point and the workbook applies another; grounds — the passage and the workbook's rule. T and O exclude each other by precedence: where the text is silent or allows more than one reading at the point, the divergence is T, whatever the workbook does;
  - **C — the reading channel:** what the builder needed was lost or altered in how its tool read the PDF; grounds — the run's channel log and the PDF's text layer at the passage;
  - **B — the builder:** the passage is unambiguous and the code departs from it; grounds — the passage and the code;
  - **P — propagated:** the divergence follows from the same builder's divergence in an earlier case (A1 → A2, A3); grounds — the isolation re-run below;
  - **U — unresolved:** no category's grounds hold; it stays unresolved.
- **The builders' agreement is not evidence:** two builders that agree can share a misreading; two that disagree are not thereby wrong.
- **Isolating a propagated divergence.** A2's and A3's curves start from A1's basic risk-free curve, which each builder computes itself. The brief asks each builder to expose its A2 and A3 computations as functions of a given basic curve; after the builds, the comparison step re-runs each builder's own A2 and A3 code — offline, in the envelope — on the official A1 curve. Those outputs are labelled «isolated» and never replace the outputs as returned.
- **Named before any build** — candidate places, none yet a divergence:

## 7. Contamination
- The text and the workbooks were published on 26 May 2026. A builder whose training data reach past that date may have read them, and what was written about them since. Each builder's stated training cutoff, against 26 May 2026: builder A's model, `gpt-6-astra` — 30 April 2026, by its vendor's model page; builder B's model, `gemini-4-argon` — March 2026, by its model card; its API page states none.
- The text post-dates both cutoffs; the method it documents does not. Three earlier public documents set that method's extrapolation and its volatility adjustment, each dated before both cutoffs, each read here at its own source — the first in its PDF from its publisher's page, the other two in their PDFs from the Official Journal:
  - EIOPA's *Opinion on the 2020 review of Solvency II* (EIOPA-BoS-20/749, 17 December 2020) advised extrapolating from the first smoothing point — 20 years for the euro at the end of 2019 — through a last liquid forward rate, a weighted average of the forward rates before and after that point weighted by the notional traded at each maturity, converging to the ultimate forward rate with a convergence parameter of 10 %, raised while rates at the first smoothing point are low and falling to 10 % by 2032; and a volatility adjustment rebuilt on the risk-corrected spread — among its parts a risk correction set as a percentage of the spread, a general application ratio of 85 %, a ratio for the mismatch between the sensitivity of fixed-income assets and that of liabilities, and a macro-economic part by country.
  - Directive (EU) 2025/2 of 27 November 2024 (OJ L, 2025/2, 8.1.2025) replaced Article 77a of Directive 2009/138/EC — the extrapolated forward rate a weighted average of a liquid forward rate and the ultimate forward rate, the ultimate forward rate's weight at least 77.5 % from 40 years past the first smoothing point, that point 20 years for the euro on 28 January 2025, and an optional phasing-in to 1 January 2032 — and paragraphs 1 to 4 of its Article 77d: the volatility adjustment as 85 % of the credit spread sensitivity ratio times the risk-corrected spread, and a macro volatility adjustment for the euro by country.
  - Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/269 of 29 October 2025 (OJ L, 2026/269, 18.2.2026) amended Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/35: in its Article 46, the formulas of the extrapolated rates, the last liquid forward rate's weights — the average notional of the interest rate swaps traded at each maturity — and the convergence parameter, 11 % (40 % for the Swedish krona); in a new Article 46a, the phasing-in from 20 % in 2027 to 11 % on 1 January 2032; and, for the volatility adjustment, its Article 50, the spread's formula, its Article 51, the risk-corrected spread, and new Articles 51a and 51b, the credit spread sensitivity ratio.

  A builder may have read any of them, and what was written about them.
- So a «reproduced» is weaker evidence that the text suffices than a «not reproduced», attributed to a passage, is evidence that it does not: a builder that knows the method from these documents can fill what the text leaves open, and the comparison of outputs cannot tell which it used. The envelope gives a builder no route to them during its run (§4): what it knows of them it brings from its training.
- A cutoff date does not prove a text unread; an egress log shows only what passed the envelope; a channel log shows what a tool reports of its own reading. Each is named for what it can show, no more.

## 8. The falsifier and the stop rule
- **The claim under test, per declared case:** the published documentation suffices to reproduce the case's official outputs.
- **Per case:** *reproduced* — both builders pass; the claim stands. *Not reproduced — the text* — a divergence attributed to a passage (T), the passage named: the claim is falsified for the case. *Not reproduced — other causes* — every divergence attributed to the oracle, the channel, a builder or propagation (O, C, B, P): the claim is not falsified; the causes are stated. *Undecided* — a divergence left unresolved (U) and none attributed to the text. A builder whose results are void (below) leaves its cases *not tested* for that builder; each such case's category is read from the other builder's results alone and named as that builder's.
- **The outcome**, computed by `verify.sh` from the table and the attributions' statuses: *all reproduced* — every case reproduced; *not reproduced* — any other, each case's category and passages named. The verdict's prose quotes the category `verify.sh` computes. The third outcome — «not buildable from public inputs» — did not arise: the earlier check passed (§3).
- **The stop rule:**
  - one run per builder; a run ended by the infrastructure, not by the builder, is restarted once, always, the failure logged — its partial return archived and kept, the restart's return the one compared; the vendor's unavailability among the infrastructure's ends, shown by the run's egress log and its CLI's error output and read by command (`preregistration/envelope/SPEC.md` §6): a run it ends is restarted up to twice — three runs at most —, a third such end leaving that builder's return what exists at that moment, named as ended by the vendor; each restart a run of its own — a new run id and a fresh run folder, its own certificate authority, vault, state, logs and work —, the same image, inputs and settings by their sha256, nothing of the ended run cleaned, copied or reused, the ended run's folder and archive kept beside it, its record naming the restart; a builder's own incomplete return is its return; a run its CLI ends on its own judgment — builder B's loop detection among them — is ended by the builder, a CLI's end after its retries on the vendor's errors excepted; builder B's CLI's five rules (§4) are its harness's: a command it denied, an output it cut, a call it ran twice and a stream it cut are counted and named in the run's record, and void nothing;
  - a run whose egress log shows a domain outside the allow-list reached, whose log shows a server-side search or fetch that ran — one the egress let through — whose `/in` carried anything beyond the brief, the PDF and the bundle, or whose mounts differ from the envelope's own — `/in`, the run's public certificate, `entry.sh` and the CLI's configuration with its folder read-only, `/work`, `/logs` and `/state` writable (`preregistration/envelope/compose.yaml`) — is void and recorded as void; one clean rerun at most; a second void leaves that builder's results void, named as such;
  - a request the egress refused — another host, another model, a tool its vendor runs beyond its own servers — reached nothing: it is named in the run's record and does not void the run;
  - a run that reaches its plan's limit, its key's prepaid balance, builder B's spend cap — $55 before tax, the egress's sum at the vendor's rates of every generation it let through: an answer by its use, one cut before its use was read at a bound (`preregistration/envelope/SPEC.md` §3b) — or its time limit — 5 hours of wall time from the launch print's first line, the print's own time counted in them — returns what exists at that moment: at the deadline the return is copied and hashed before any grace period, and the archive carries that copy as the return, any later write beside it, labelled; it is not restarted; a 402 from the vendor, or a 429 naming the balance, its credits, billing, a spending cap or a quota, is the plan's limit, not the vendor's unavailability;
  - no second round of builds, and no change to the brief, the bands or the cases after the freeze; one independent review; then the dispositions and the verdict.
- One run per builder is one sample of a stochastic process: the result says what these two runs did, not how often a builder would succeed.

## 9. The independent review
One independent review, by a reviewer outside the builds. It receives the whole pass-or-fail table, the divergence log with every attribution and its grounds, the text, the brief, and the claims of this folder's README and note beside their evidence. It is committed as received before any disposition is written; the dispositions follow in a later commit. One round.

## 10. The record — five commits, each under a timestamp token
- (i) the text's pointers (`source/`), the manifest (`manifest/`) and this preregistration with the brief, the bundle's README, the envelope's spec and the tools that write and check the bundle and set the bands (`preregistration/`) — before any build; (ii) the builds as returned, their egress logs and the hashes of their full logs — before any comparison; (iii) the comparison tool, the pass-or-fail table and the divergence log; (iv) the review as received — before any disposition; (v) the dispositions, the model spend, the READMEs and the note, `verify.sh`, the hash chain and the licence.
- **Each commit's token:** a file list — `mode  sha256  path` of every file the commit carries, its mode as git records it, sorted bytewise by path — whose sha256 is the imprint of an RFC 3161 token from a public timestamp authority; the list's first line names the list's and the token's own paths, which it leaves out, so that no token covers its own bytes; `verify.sh` checks token, list and files against each other. A token dates the files' bytes, paths and modes as committed, not the acts before them.
- **Before each commit, a leak check** reads every file the commit carries — as UTF-16 where it carries a byte-order mark or its NUL bytes fall at alternate positions — and runs every class on the file's path as on its body: for the first workbook's input values — each one written with four or more significant digits, or as an integer with five or more — as given, in per cent and in basis points, a leading decimal point read as a number, and a number of twelve or more digits also when it lies within a relative 10⁻¹² of one of them — finite numbers alone: a number past the float range is near none; for any run of twelve words of the text's text layer, six or more of them words of three or more letters, the running header on its pages — the document's name — left out; for the persons the publisher's files name in their metadata; for a copy or a near-copy of any of the four files — a file equal to a member of a workbook, or a window of a file's bytes — and of a bundle file other than its README — any of its lines, or an ordered run of one input range's values long enough to carry 48 bits; for the shapes of API keys and account identifiers, in the case their issuers write them; and for a list of terms the author keeps out of this repository, each matched in any case. An archive is opened and each of its members checked as a file of its own, its name as its path — a ZIP wherever in the file it begins, a tar, a gzip, bzip2 or xz file read through all its streams; a tar member that is not a regular file by its name and a link's target, a ZIP entry named as a directory read as any other member — and a member that is itself an archive is opened in turn, three levels of archives in all. Nothing is passed unread in silence: a member is read to 128 MiB and one byte past it, its declared size not trusted, and a compressed file's streams to 128 MiB together; a member or a file past that size, by its declared or its actual size; a damaged archive, member or stream — a read or a listing that fails, a compressed stream cut short, or a ZIP member compressed by deflate, bzip2 or LZMA whose compressed stream does not end within its declared compressed size or leaves some of those bytes after its end; an encrypted member; an archive at a fourth level; bytes after a compressed file's last stream; and the bytes of a ZIP that no entry declares — from its first physical local header, the first local-header signature its bytes hold, to its central directory, any byte outside every entry's local header, declared data and data descriptor, and the whole span when its directory lists no entry while local headers lie before it — are each a hit of their own, and stop the commit as any hit does. Its deny-list is built at each run from the pinned files, the bundle held to its pinned sha256 list, and is never written here. A hit stops the commit (§2). Zero hits is not proof that nothing slipped through — a near-copy that rewrites every line and rounds or re-spells its values passes it, and so do a range too short to carry such a run and a file in a format the standard library does not recognise as an archive or a compressed stream — 7z, rar or zstd among them, or a compressed stream that does not begin its file — which is read as bytes only: the author reads every file before each command. The check and its controls carry a second party's check before the freeze. The builds' full logs and their archives stay out of this repository: they hold what the builders read.

## 11. What this test does not show
- No regulatory-accountability claim of any kind; nothing about the publisher's production curves, whose inputs are licensed market data; nothing about the shocks this text does not carry.
- A divergence attributed to a passage is a reading the passage allows, set beside the publisher's own example — never an error of the text's owner.
- Two runs, two builders, six cases: no rate of success, no ranking of model families.
- A «reproduced» does not show that the text alone is enough to rebuild the extrapolation and the volatility adjustment it documents: both were public before the builders' training cutoffs (§7).
- The method is not compared with human reviewers or with established providers.

## 12. Verifying this folder
`verify.sh`, standard library only: fetches the four files by URL and checks their sha256; re-derives the manifest's digests; checks every hash of the chain and every token; re-derives the pass-or-fail table from the archived builds and the oracle with the checked comparison tool, called by its sha256; computes the outcome. It does not re-run the builders — a builder run again writes other code; what is verified is the archive — and it cannot judge an attribution's grounds: the reader does.
